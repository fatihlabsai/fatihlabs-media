# The AI That Grades Its Own Homework (working title)

## Script
[0-3 s] HOOK: This AI does its own homework, grades itself, then trains itself. What could go wrong?

[3-45 s] MAIN: Japan's Sakana AI tested a method called MASS. One model does three jobs. One: solve the task. Two: score its own work. Three: retrain on its best-scoring attempts. The result? Better at research tasks, but not everywhere. The catch: if it misses a mistake both while solving and grading, it thinks that mistake is a success, and could learn it even better. So its blind spot can get reinforced over time. The researchers flag this as a risk too. Sound familiar? When you check your own writing or plans, what slips past you once slips past you again.

[45-60 s] CLOSE: The lesson: on anything important, let someone else grade you too. Who did you last ask for honest feedback? Comment below, and follow for more.

## On-screen text
- The AI that does its own homework and grades it
- 1) Solve  2) Score  3) Retrain itself
- Better at research... but
- It mistakes its blind spots for "success"
- Grade yourself, and you miss your own mistakes
- Get a second grader
- Who did you last ask for feedback?

## Visual ideas
- Opening: a robot writing "100" on its own paper in red pen (animation)
- Loop diagram for the three steps: Solve -> Score -> Retrain -> back to start
- Mistake moment: a small red spot in the loop that rides along unnoticed each round and stays put (do not show it growing; the message is "can be reinforced")

- Human connection: someone reads and approves their own writing, then a friend points out the mistake
- Close: two people checking each other's papers + follow/comment call to action
